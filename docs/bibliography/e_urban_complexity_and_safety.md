# **E. Urban complexity, risk assessment, and formal safety**

Four papers covering the broader autonomous-driving context: urban complexity (Wei), risk-assessment surveys (Lefèvre), formal-safety frameworks (Shalev-Shwartz), and interaction-aware planning (Brito). The Social Latency penalty in week 10 is informally aligned with the interaction-aware framing in Brito et al.

**[16] **B. Brito, A. Agarwal, and J. Alonso-Mora, "Learning interaction-aware guidance policies for motion planning in dense traffic scenarios," in IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), 2020, pp. 1730-1736.

***Used in this project: ****Cited in the March Monthly Report as the closest neighbor to the project's Social Latency reward shaping. Their interaction-aware guidance framing is conceptually adjacent to our **"**reward the agent for not making neighbors more aggressive.**"** Read in week 9 during the dose-response sweep planning.*

**[17] **S. Lefèvre, D. Vasquez, and C. Laugier, "A survey on motion prediction and risk assessment for intelligent vehicles," ROBOMECH Journal, vol. 1, no. 1, pp. 1-14, 2014.

***Used in this project: ****Used as the broader motion-prediction context for the Final Report's discussion section. The closed-form AI score is positioned as a risk-assessment signal that could plug into the family of approaches Lefèvre surveys.*

**[18] **S. Shalev-Shwartz, S. Shammah, and A. Shashua, "On a formal model of safe and scalable self-driving cars," arXiv preprint arXiv:1708.06374, 2017.

***Used in this project: ****[seminal] The Responsibility-Sensitive Safety (RSS) paper. Cited in the Final Report's limitations section as the safety floor that any deployed system would need to comply with on top of the AI score. Our approach is descriptive (measuring aggressiveness), RSS is prescriptive (defining safe behavior); the two are complementary.*

**[19] **J. Wei, J. M. Snider, T. Gu, J. M. Dolan, and B. Litkouhi, "Autonomous driving in urban environments: The role of anticipation and interaction," Proceedings of the IEEE, vol. 102, no. 6, pp. 945-958, 2014.

***Used in this project: ****Background reference for urban driving complexity. Cited in the Milestone-1 report's literature review for the broader framing of why urban environments require richer behavioral models than highway scenarios — directly relevant to the Urban regime in the multi-environment AI formulation.*
